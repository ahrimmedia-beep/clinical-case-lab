Dear Dr. Patel,

Thank you for referring this 44-year-old woman with progressive breathlessness over 3 years. She can now walk only about 100 yards on the flat before stopping. She has a morning cough productive of clear sputum. She denies hemoptysis and ankle swelling. She uses a salbutamol inhaler as needed, with little benefit.

She is a former smoker of ten pack-years who quit 8 years ago, and she works as a school administrator. Raised liver enzymes were noted on routine blood tests in 2021 and were not investigated further. Her only other history is a right carpal tunnel release in 2020. The family history is striking: her brother was diagnosed with liver cirrhosis in his forties despite drinking very little, and her father died of emphysema at 52.

On examination her chest was hyperinflated, with reduced breath sounds at both bases. SpO2 95% on air.

Investigations: post-bronchodilator FEV1 1.4 L, FEV1/FVC 0.48, TLCO 55% predicted. CT chest shows panlobular emphysema, predominantly in the lower lobes. Serum alpha-1 antitrypsin 25 mg/dL (low; reference 90-200). Genotyping confirmed PiZZ. ALT 58 U/L (raised).

Impression: severe alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency (PiZZ) with lower-lobe panlobular emphysema. Smoking-related COPD alone would be unusual with only ten pack-years and this distribution. Asthma was considered, but she has no history of atopy and the obstruction persists after bronchodilator.

Plan: refer to the regional AATD centre to discuss augmentation therapy; start a LAMA/LABA inhaler; influenza and pneumococcal vaccination; liver ultrasound and elastography; offer testing to her siblings and children.

Yours sincerely,
Dr. R. Castellanos, Consultant Respiratory Physician

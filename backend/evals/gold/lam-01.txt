PULMONARY CONSULTATION

Reason for consultation: recurrent pneumothorax and progressive breathlessness.

History of present illness: A thirty-eight-year-old woman is referred after her second right-sided spontaneous pneumothorax in 14 months. She reports progressive dyspnea on exertion over 2 years and now has to stop after one flight of stairs. She also describes an intermittent dry cough. She denies hemoptysis, fever or weight loss. She had a urinary tract infection last spring that resolved with a course of nitrofurantoin.

Past history: R PTX 14 months ago, treated with a chest tube. Renal angiomyolipoma found incidentally on abdominal ultrasound in 2023.
Medications: combined oral contraceptive pill. No known drug allergies.
Social: lifelong non-smoker; works as a schoolteacher.

Examination: SpO2 ninety-four percent on room air, RR 18/min, HR 88 bpm. Breath sounds are reduced at the right base. No clubbing.

Investigations: FEV1 1.9 L, FEV1/FVC 0.61, DLCO 48% of predicted. HRCT chest shows numerous thin-walled round cysts distributed diffusely throughout both lungs and a small residual right apical pneumothorax. Serum VEGF-D 1,240 pg/mL. CT abdomen confirms a 2.1 cm left renal angiomyolipoma.

Impression: lymphangioleiomyomatosis (LAM), sporadic form, given the diffuse cysts, renal angiomyolipoma and VEGF-D above 800 pg/mL. Pulmonary Langerhans cell histiocytosis was considered but is unlikely in a lifelong non-smoker. Birt-Hogg-Dube syndrome remains in the differential until genetic testing is back.

Plan: start sirolimus; stop the combined oral contraceptive pill; discuss pleurodesis.

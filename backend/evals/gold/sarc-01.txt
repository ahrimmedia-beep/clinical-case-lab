RESPIRATORY OUTPATIENT CONSULTATION

Reason for referral: persistent cough and an abnormal chest CT.

History: This 36-year-old woman has had a dry cough for 4 months and exertional dyspnea that now stops her on the second flight of stairs. Three weeks ago she noticed painful red lumps on both shins, followed by pain in both ankles that makes walking difficult in the morning. She denies night sweats, weight loss or fever. She has no visual symptoms and no palpitations.

Past history: hypothyroidism (Hashimoto thyroiditis) diagnosed in 2018, stable on levothyroxine 75 mcg daily. Tonsillectomy in childhood.
Social: lifelong never-smoker; works as a librarian. No known TB contacts and no exposure to birds or moulds.

Examination: SpO2 97% on room air, HR 76 bpm. Tender erythematous nodules on the anterior aspect of both shins. Periarticular swelling and tenderness of both ankles. No crackles on auscultation. No peripheral lymphadenopathy.

Investigations: Serum ACE 98 U/L (raised). Adjusted calcium 2.71 mmol/L (high). PFTs: FVC 78% predicted, DLCO 69% predicted. IGRA negative. CT chest: bilateral hilar and mediastinal lymphadenopathy with perilymphatic nodules along the bronchovascular bundles in both upper lobes. EBUS-TBNA of the subcarinal and right hilar nodes showed non-caseating epithelioid granulomas, with no malignant cells.

Impression: sarcoidosis, Scadding stage II, presenting as Löfgren syndrome (erythema nodosum, bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy and ankle periarthritis). Tuberculosis is unlikely with a negative IGRA and no caseation on cytology. Lymphoma was considered because of the nodal enlargement but is not supported by the cytology.

Plan: NSAIDs for the ankle symptoms; ophthalmology review and ECG; avoid vitamin D supplements; repeat calcium and lung function in 3 months; no systemic corticosteroids for now.
